#  Post-Mortem: Yesterday's Puke

Before I get into it — I worked through this with my AI buddy, "Claude" to help me put structure around what went wrong. The discretionary lessons are mine, but the framework I'm about to propose (UER and FER) came out of that conversation as a way to turn a fuzzy feeling of "too much fresh risk" into actual numbers I can track in my sheet. Sharing it because it might be useful for others running similar setups with soft SLs.

---

Yesterday hurt. Not because I took a hit — taking hits is part of the game — but because of *how* I took it. I gave back all my Open Heat in a single session after an incredible run, and worse, I had to close core positions that were still trading above structure just to manage aggregate risk. That's the part I refuse to replicate.

Let me be honest with myself about what happened.

## What Went Right Before It Went Wrong

The run was real. The cores were working — ARM, NBIS, COHR, MU, the trimmed APLD — these were textbook. Entries near structure, trims along the way, secured profits banking up. That's the system doing exactly what it's supposed to do. I had earned the right to be aggressive, and the tape was rewarding me for it.

The problem isn't that I added exposure. The problem is *how* I added it and *when*.

## The Discretionary Mistake

I got aggressive with fresh exposure into a market that was extended and after an already big run. INTC, NVDA, APLD, TSLA, BE — all entered within the last few sessions, all stacked on top of an already heavy book at 172.5% gross exposure. I was pressing into strength instead of letting the cores do the work.

This is the part I own as a judgment call. I read the tape as "still working" instead of "running out of room." Markets that have already moved a lot don't need me adding more fresh risk — they need me protecting what's already on. I knew this. I did it anyway because the run made me feel like every entry was going to work.

That's discretion, that's psychology, that's something I have to keep working on. Fine. Hard lesson, but a lesson.

## The Structural Mistake — This One Is Not Discretionary

Here's where it gets useful, because this part is fixable with data. But before I get into the numbers, let me explain what I'm actually going to start tracking.

**UER — Unproven Exposure Ratio.** It's the percentage of my book sitting in positions with P&L less than 5%. These are trades that haven't built a profit cushion yet. UER answers the question: *"How green is my book?"* High UER means I've been stacking too many fresh entries and the book hasn't had time to mature.

**FER — Fragile Exposure Ratio.** It's the percentage of my book with thin cushion to its SL — current price within 5% of where my stop sits — regardless of whether the position is proven or fresh. I'm using 5% because my average stock ATR is around 5%+, so anything inside that range is essentially within one daily move of stopping out. FER answers a different question: *"How much of my book stops out on a normal bad day?"* It catches both fresh tight-cushion entries AND proven positions where I've raised the SL up tight to current price.

The two metrics cover different blind spots. UER is about *P&L history*. FER is about *distance to stop right now*. A book can be mature (low UER) but still fragile if SLs are raised tight across the board. A book can be fresh (high UER) but safe if those new entries have plenty of room to their SLs. The danger is when both are high at the same time — fresh positions clustered tight against their stops.

## What My Book Actually Looked Like Going Into Thursday's Close

When I run the actual numbers on Thursday's close prices, here's what was sitting in front of me:

**UER was around 50%** — half my exposure was in positions with less than 5% P&L. INTC, NVDA, APLD, TSLA, BE were all fresh, and several of the older fresh entries hadn't built much cushion yet either.

**FER was sitting at roughly 47%** — almost half the book within 5% of its SL on Thursday's close.

And here's the part that really shows the failure mode: the fragile bucket wasn't spread evenly. It was concentrated almost entirely in **INTC (both layers), NVDA (both layers), and TSLA (all four layers)**. All semis and high-beta names. All clustered. All with tight cushions to their respective 21dma-low SLs:

- NVDA: ~2.9% cushion
- INTC: ~5% cushion (right at the edge)
- TSLA: ~1.4% cushion

The other positions — COHR, MU, ARM, NBIS, BE, CAT — had healthy room thanks to prior trims or large P&L moves pushing them well above their stops. They were never the problem.

Both thresholds are at 25% because that's the level where the book still has a clear majority of proven, well-cushioned positions doing the work — anything higher and the unproven or fragile layer starts to dictate the outcome of the whole portfolio.

These aren't discretionary judgments. They're math. They're things I can put in my sheet, check every morning, and use as a checklist  before clicking buy.

Next iteration I'll likely refine FER to use **1× ATR per stock** instead of a flat 5%, so each position is measured against its own volatility profile rather than a portfolio average — but the flat version is good enough to ship today and would have caught yesterday clearly.

## The Honest Takeaway

I had a great run. I let the success make me a little too eager to keep pressing. The market gave me a normal-bad day — not a black swan, just a tail event that happens a few times a year — and my book structure couldn't absorb it because UER and FER were both elevated at the same time, with the fragility concentrated in the most correlated names in my book.

The good news: I'm not broken. The system isn't broken. Soft SLs aren't broken. What's broken is the lack of a guardrail that tells me when my book is too top-heavy with fresh risk and too tight on cushion at the same time. That's a fixable problem, and I'm fixing it.

The trades I lost — fine. The cores I had to puke — that's the one that stings, and that's the one I'm building this for.

So when QQQ delivered a -3 ATR move (call it 2.5-3σ, a genuine tail day but not a once-a-decade event), what happened was completely predictable:

- The fragile cluster (semis + Tesla) blew through their soft SLs almost simultaneously — they had no room AND they were the highest-beta names in the book, so they moved the most on the down day.
- The cores held up fine because they had cushion from prior trims and gains.
- But the aggregate Open Heat from that fragile cluster stopping out pushed me to my full Open Heat limit.
- To manage risk, I had no choice but to puke the cores too — positions that were *still above structure* and didn't deserve to die.

That's the failure mode. It wasn't the cores that killed me. It wasn't even any single fresh position individually. It was the **clustering** — too many fragile entries piled on top of each other in correlated names, entered close in time, all triggering together on the same -3 ATR day. Both UER and FER were flashing red, I just didn't have the metrics yet to see it.

## What I Refuse to Replicate

Taking a stop on a fresh position because the trade didn't work — that's fine. That's the cost of doing business.

Closing a core position that's still above structure because I mismanaged my fresh exposure stack — that's not acceptable. Those cores were my edge. They were the proven trades. Killing them to save the unproven ones is exactly backwards.

## The Fix

The discretionary part — being more patient about adding fresh exposure into extended markets — I'll keep working on. That's a slow lesson.

The structural part is where I can install real guardrails *right now*. The rules I'm putting in place:

- **UER > 25%** → no new fresh entries; let the book mature first
- **FER > 25%** → no new entries at all; the book is too tight regardless of P&L

Either one flashing red is enough to pause. Each closes a different door — UER stops me from stacking fresh exposure, FER stops me from adding when the book overall is one bad session from stopping out.
